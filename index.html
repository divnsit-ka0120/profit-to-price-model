📐 The Mental Model expand ▼
Price = EPS × P/E
Layer 1 — Earnings Growth (the engine): If a company grows profit at 20% CAGR and P/E stays the same, stock price also grows at 20% CAGR. This is the compounding engine.
Layer 2 — Multiple Re-rating (the turbo): If the market starts valuing the stock at a higher P/E (because growth is accelerating, or sentiment improves), price jumps even more. A stock going from 15x to 25x P/E gives you a 67% bonus on top of earnings growth.
Layer 3 — Multiple Compression (the risk): If growth slows, the P/E shrinks. Even if EPS is growing at 15%, a P/E drop from 40x to 20x will halve your return. This is how "expensive" stocks punish you.
PEG Ratio = P/E ÷ Growth Rate. PEG < 1 means you're getting growth cheaply. PEG > 2 means you're overpaying. It's a quick sanity check.

Quick Rules of Thumb
Constant P/E: Your return ≈ EPS growth rate. 20% profit CAGR → ~20% stock CAGR.
P/E expansion: Bonus multiplier. Usually happens when growth surprises to the upside or rates fall.
P/E compression: The risk. High P/E stocks need sustained high growth. Any slowdown → double hit.
PEG sweet spot: PEG between 0.5–1.0 = getting growth at a reasonable price. Above 2 = priced for perfection.
